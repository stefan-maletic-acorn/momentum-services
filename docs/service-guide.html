1About this guide

{{service.summary}}

This guide explains how that service is delivered and supported. It sets out what happens at each stage between order and go-live, the three periods of protection that begin on the go-live date, what Momentum Care covers and what it does not, and the service levels we hold ourselves to when something goes wrong.

It is written for the people who live with the workflow after it is built: the business owner who sponsored it, the administrators who run it day to day, and the manager who signs the renewal. It is also the reference our Solutions Architects work from, so both sides are reading the same page.

It describes the standard service. Your quote and order name the workflows, the tier of each, the Care tier and the term. Where an order includes a position that differs from this guide, the order applies.

3Delivery standards

Delivery runs to a fixed shape whatever the workflow. The stages below are the same for a Simple, Moderate or Complex workflow. What changes is the length of the build sprint and the amount of design there is to confirm.

3.1Pre-scope and Discovery

Pre-scope is a free 45-minute call before any quote. It scores the workflow against the Complexity Scorecard and produces a tier and a price. It is not a design engagement and produces no deliverable beyond those two things. Where a factor is unclear on the call, it scores high, so a pre-scope price is a ceiling more often than a floor.

Discovery. {{discovery.text}} It produces four things:

    {{block:discovery_deliverables}}

The signed Workflow Design Document is the baseline for everything that follows. Build is measured against it, UAT tests against it, warranty defects are defined by it, and Care maintains what it describes. Anything not in the signed design is not in scope, however sensible it sounds.

Discovery re-scores the workflow with full information. If the confirmed tier differs from the pre-scope tier, one adjustment is made at the end of Discovery, in writing, and only the difference between the two tiers is billed or credited. There is no second adjustment: after Discovery the tier is fixed, and any change that would move it is handled as a build increment under section 7.

3.2Build

Build is a single sprint of configuration in your staging environment, followed by our own testing before anything reaches you. A Simple workflow takes {{build.duration.simple}}, a Moderate one {{build.duration.moderate}}, a Complex one {{build.duration.complex}}. The sprint is scheduled in Discovery against the Acorn release calendar and the go-live date is set at the same time.

The sprint clock

A sprint has a start date and an end date, and your go-live slot is booked on the strength of them. The clock runs while we have what we need from you. When we are waiting on an answer, a test user or a piece of data for more than three business days, the clock stops, and it restarts when the item arrives. A stopped clock does not extend the sprint; it moves the whole schedule, including the go-live slot, to the next release window that fits.

A sprint can be deferred before it starts. {{terms.9.position}} The fee reflects a Solutions Architect held for a slot that cannot be resold at short notice.

3.3User acceptance testing and rework

UAT is yours to run, and it runs for five business days from the demonstration at the end of the build. You test the workflow in staging against the signed design document, using the test users and data you set up before Discovery closed, and covering every role and every approval path. We support you through it: we answer questions, explain behaviour and help reproduce anything unexpected.

Defects come back to us as one consolidated list at the end of the window rather than one at a time. A defect is anything the workflow does that the signed design says it should not, or anything the design says it should do that it does not. Something the design does not mention is not a defect; it is a change request, and it goes through the process in 3.5.

We then make one rework pass, fixing everything on the list, and you confirm the fixes and sign off. Sign-off is the trigger for go-live. A UAT window that drifts past five days, or a defect list that arrives in pieces, pushes go-live to the next release slot, because the deployment is booked against the release calendar and cannot float.

3.4Go-live

Go-live is the deployment of the signed-off workflow into production against the release calendar. It comes with a handover pack and a sixty-minute administrator walkthrough. The handover pack contains the workflow documentation, the full form question set, and the escalation map showing who receives what and when. The walkthrough covers how to read the live table of requests, how to find a stuck request, what the administrators may change themselves and, just as importantly, what they should ask us to change so that the workflow stays covered.

The go-live date is the date that matters commercially and for service. Hypercare, warranty and Care all start on it. The second half of the Build fee and the first year of Care are invoiced on it.

3.5Change during delivery

Good ideas surface in every demonstration and every UAT. They are welcome, and they are candidates, not commitments. A change to the signed design is raised through the agreed channel as a change request, assessed against the scorecard, and either priced from the rate card, added as a build increment, or parked for the Care allowance once the workflow is live. Nothing said in a demo changes the design until it is written down and agreed.

This is not bureaucracy for its own sake. The build is fixed price against a fixed design, and the go-live slot is fixed against the release calendar. Changing the design mid-build puts both at risk, and the change is nearly always cheaper and safer as a small increment after go-live.

4The Care period

{{care.text}}

The period after go-live is where most of the value of the service is delivered, and it is where the boundaries matter most. Three protections begin on the go-live date. Two of them expire; one renews.

4.1Hypercare, the first ten business days

The first real requests through a new workflow find the things no test plan does: a manager relationship that was never populated, a notification that reads oddly to the person receiving it, a reviewer who did not realise they were a reviewer. Hypercare is ten business days of heightened attention to catch them quickly.

During hypercare, any issue we assess as Critical is handled at the fastest response time in the service, {{derived.fastest_critical}}, regardless of your Care tier. We watch the live table of requests each business day for anything stuck or routed to the wrong person, and we tell you before you have to tell us. Fixes made in hypercare draw nothing from your Care allowance.

Hypercare is included in the Build fee. It is not a second UAT window: it does not reopen the design, and requests for new behaviour raised during it are handled as change requests.

4.2Warranty, the first thirty business days

Warranty is our commitment that the workflow does what the signed design document says it does. For thirty business days from go-live, any defect measured against the signed design is fixed at no charge, at the service levels of your Care tier, without drawing on your Care allowance.

A warranty defect is one where the workflow's behaviour and the signed design disagree. The following are not warranty defects, and are handled as Care requests or change requests instead:

  • Behaviour the signed design does not describe, or describes as you now realise you did not want.
  • Routing that fails because a data dependency on the register was not populated as agreed, or has since changed.
  • Anything altered by your own administrators in the Builder after go-live.
  • Defects in the Momentum platform itself, which Acorn Support handles under your license.

Warranty is included in the Build fee. It overlaps with hypercare for its first ten business days and with Care for all of them.

4.3Momentum Care, ongoing

Care starts on the go-live date and runs for twelve months at a time, renewing with your Momentum license so that the two never fall out of step. {{terms.8.position}} The fee is invoiced annually in advance, the first year on go-live, and increases by {{indexation.cpi_percent}}% at each anniversary.

{{terms.7.position}} A workflow built by Acorn is covered by Care at Essential or above for as long as it runs in your tenant. If Care is not renewed, the workflow keeps running, but it has no service levels, no release compatibility check and no allowance, and Acorn is no longer responsible for its behaviour. Bringing it back under Care later requires a re-baseline at the rate card, so it is materially cheaper to keep Care continuous.

5.1Coverage window

Standard coverage is {{care.coverage}} Service level clocks run only inside this window, and a request received outside it is treated as received at the start of the next covered hour. Section 8.3 shows how that works in practice.

{{care.extended_coverage.text}} Extended coverage widens the window in which the clocks run and in which a Solutions Architect will pick up a Critical issue. It does not change the response, workaround or resolution targets themselves, and it does not cover weekends or public holidays.

5.2The change allowance

Each tier includes a number of Solutions Architect hours a year, per workflow, for small changes that keep the workflow matched to your organisation: reworded notifications, a new option on a form question, an adjusted reminder interval. The allowance is what makes Care more than break-fix.

  • What draws on it. Changes you ask for that alter what the workflow says or how a setting is tuned, without adding structure. Section 6 marks each inclusion as drawing on the allowance or not.
  • What does not. Break-fix, release compatibility work, drift correction, reassignment of constants and roles, incident coordination, and anything done under hypercare or warranty. These are the service, not the allowance.
  • How it is counted. In quarter-hour increments of Solutions Architect time, from the moment we start the change to the moment it is confirmed in production, including the testing.
  • When it runs out. Further changes in that year are quoted from the rate card at {{money:rate_card.hourly}} an hour, or held for the next allowance year if they can wait. We tell you when you are at three-quarters of the allowance, and again when it is exhausted.
  • Validity. {{care.allowance_note}} The allowance is per workflow and cannot be pooled across workflows or transferred between them.

5.3Reviews and health checks

Every tier includes a regular look at the workflow's health, at a cadence that rises with the tier.

  • Annual health report (Essential). A written report at each anniversary: request volumes and completion times, requests that stalled and where, constants and roles that point to people who have left or moved, releases applied in the year and what each needed, allowance used, and recommendations.
  • Quarterly health check (Standard). The same report every quarter, with a thirty-minute call to walk through it and agree any allowance work for the coming quarter.
  • Monthly review with a named Solutions Architect (Premier). A named Solutions Architect who knows your workflows, a monthly review call, and a standing view of the quarter's changes and the release calendar ahead. Your named architect is the first escalation point for anything Critical or High.

6What Care includes

Care covers six kinds of work. Each entry below gives the published position, what it means in practice, and whether the work draws on your allowance. Where a service level applies, the severity it is usually assessed at is shown so you know what to expect.

Break-fix on any defect in an Acorn-built workflow

{{care.covered.0}}

If a workflow we built stops doing what its signed design says, we fix it. That includes a transition that no longer fires, a form that will not submit, an escalation that fails to trigger after its wait, a routing rule that sends a request to the wrong person because the rule is wrong, and a notification that goes out with the wrong content or to the wrong recipient. Break-fix continues after warranty for as long as Care runs.

We diagnose in production data where we can and reproduce in staging where we cannot. Where the cause is a platform defect rather than the workflow, we hand it to Acorn Support under your license and stay on the incident until it is resolved, see section 7.

7What Care excludes

Care keeps a workflow doing what it was designed to do. It does not grow it, and it does not cover work outside the signed design. Each exclusion below says why it sits outside Care and how it is handled instead, so that nothing is a surprise when it comes up.

New nodes, branches, forms or approval tiers

{{care.not_covered.0}}

Adding a step, a new path out of a step, a new form or another level of approval changes the structure the workflow was scored, priced and tested on. Each new element carries its own design, configuration, notification templates, role scoping and a test from every side, which is exactly the work Build is priced for.

Instead: raised as a change request. Small additions are quoted from the rate card. Larger additions are re-scored against the scorecard and quoted as a build increment with its own mini Discovery, build, UAT and go-live, and are then covered by Care from the day they go live.

8Service levels

Service levels describe how quickly we respond to, work around and resolve an issue with a workflow under Care, by the severity of that issue. They apply from go-live, run inside the coverage window in section 5.1, and are the same for every workflow tier. The Care tier sets the Critical response time and whether service credits apply.

8.1Severity definitions and targets

{{block:service_levels}}

Critical response is {{care.tiers.0.critical_response}} on Essential, {{care.tiers.1.critical_response}} on Standard and {{care.tiers.2.critical_response}} on Premier. During hypercare every workflow gets {{derived.fastest_critical}} regardless of tier.

  • Critical. Nobody can submit, approve or progress requests through the workflow at all, and the process it serves has a statutory, safety, payroll or hard-deadline consequence. A leave request workflow down on the payroll cut-off day is Critical. The same workflow down on a quiet Tuesday with two weeks to cut-off is High.
  • High. A core function is broken but the process can still complete, with effort. Escalations not firing, a request routed to the wrong approver, notifications not sending so people do not know they have something to do. Requests can still be moved on by hand, so it is not Critical, but every day it persists costs you time.
  • Medium. One user, one request or one path is affected, or the problem is cosmetic. A form label that is misleading, a single request that stalled once, a notification with a typo in the subject line.
  • Low / request. Not a defect. A configuration change within the Care allowance, raised through the normal channel. The resolution target is the next sprint slot, because allowance changes are batched into scheduled work, or fifteen business days at the outside.

8.2What each clock measures

  • Response is the time from your request reaching the agreed channel inside the coverage window to a Solutions Architect acknowledging it, confirming the severity, and telling you what happens next. It is a person, not an automatic receipt.
  • Workaround is the time to a state where the process can complete again, even if not elegantly: requests reassigned by hand, a path temporarily bypassed, a notification sent manually. The workaround stops the harm while the fix is built.
  • Resolution is the time to the underlying cause being fixed in production and confirmed, or, for a Low request, the change being made. Where a resolution needs a platform release, the resolution target applies to the workaround and the platform fix follows the release calendar.

All three clocks start at the same moment and run concurrently. They are measured in business hours and business days inside the coverage window, so a target of one business day is {{derived.hours_in_day}} covered hours, not 24 elapsed hours.

8.3Business hours in practice

The coverage window is {{care.coverage}} That is {{derived.hours_in_day}} business hours a day. A request raised at 3:00pm is due a response as follows.

Critical on {{derived.slowest_tier}}, {{derived.slowest_critical}}{{derived.clock_slow}}Two and a half hours remain in the day; the rest carries into the next morning.
Critical on {{derived.fastest_tier}}, {{derived.fastest_critical}}{{derived.clock_fast}}Inside the same coverage window.
High, {{service_levels.1.response}}{{derived.clock_high}}The same arithmetic, on every tier.

A request raised at 7:00pm on a Friday without extended coverage is treated as received at 8:30am on Monday, and the clocks start then. A request raised on a national public holiday or during the Acorn end-of-year shutdown starts on the next covered day. With extended coverage on Premier, the window is 7:00am to 7:00pm and the same arithmetic applies to the wider day.

8.4When the clocks pause

The clocks measure our time, not everybody's. They pause, and restart where they left off, when:

  • We have asked you for information, access or a decision we need to proceed, until it arrives.
  • The issue is a platform defect and has been handed to Acorn Support, for the resolution clock only. The workaround clock keeps running.
  • The coverage window closes, until it reopens.
  • You have asked us to hold, for example to avoid a change during a live approval round.

Service levels are suspended altogether on a workflow that has been changed in the Builder by someone other than Acorn, until a re-baseline is complete, as set out in section 7.

8.5Assigning severity and escalating

You tell us how urgent something is when you raise it. The Solutions Architect confirms the severity at response against the definitions in 8.1, and tells you if it differs from what you asked for and why. Severity can be raised or lowered as the situation changes: a High issue that is still open on the morning of a statutory deadline becomes Critical.

If you believe an issue is being handled at the wrong severity, or a target has been missed, escalate to the named Solutions Architect on Premier or to the Momentum Services lead on any tier. The escalation is acknowledged within the response time for the severity you believe applies.

8.6Service credits

Service credits apply on {{derived.credit_tiers}}, and not on {{derived.no_credit_tiers}}. Where we miss a resolution target on a Critical or High issue and the clock was running, a credit against the next Care invoice is applied on the schedule set out in your order. Credits are the remedy for a missed target; they are not payable where the clock was paused under 8.4, where service levels were suspended under section 7, or where the issue is a platform defect being resolved by Acorn Support.

9Raising a request

Everything under Care, whether an incident or a change, is raised through the agreed channel named in your order. One channel means one queue, one record and one set of clocks. A request mentioned in a review call or a hallway conversation is a candidate until it is in the channel.

A good request lets us start work without a round trip. Include:

  • The workflow's name, and the request or instance number from the live table if one request is affected.
  • What you expected to happen and what happened instead, with a screenshot where it helps.
  • Who is affected and what is waiting on it, so we can confirm severity quickly.
  • For a change, the exact new wording or setting you want, and whether it can wait for the next scheduled slot.
  • Who to contact, and the best way to reach them if the issue is Critical.

We acknowledge the request within the response time for its severity, and we keep you updated: at least daily on a Critical issue while it is open, at each milestone on a High issue, and at resolution on Medium issues and Low requests. Every request is closed with a note saying what was done, and change requests record the allowance used.

12Definitions

Workflow
One Momentum process built in your tenant from a signed design: its nodes, transitions, forms, constants, routing rules and notifications together.
Node or step
One box on the Builder canvas: a form somebody fills in, an automatic email, a wait, a hand-off or an end.
Transition or path
One way a request can leave a step, including approve, reject, send-back and loop-back paths.
Constant
A named value the workflow routes or refers to, most often a person or inbox, set once and used by many steps.
Role scope
Who can see or act on a form or a step. Each scope is configured and tested from both sides.
Signed design
The Workflow Design Document as signed at the end of Discovery, plus any later re-baseline or build increment signed since.
Complexity tier
Simple, Moderate or Complex, set by the eight-factor Complexity Scorecard and confirmed in Discovery.
Care tier
Essential, Standard or Premier. One Care tier applies to every workflow on an order.
Allowance
The Solutions Architect hours a year, per workflow, included in a Care tier for changes within the signed design.
Business hours and days
Hours and days inside the coverage window in section 5.1. {{derived.hours_in_day}} business hours make one business day.
Release calendar
Acorn's published schedule of platform releases, against which go-lives and sprint slots are booked.
Re-baseline
A rate-card review that brings the design document and the live configuration back into agreement after a change Acorn did not make, restoring service levels.
Build increment
A scoped, priced addition to a live workflow: its own design, build, UAT and go-live, then covered by Care.
Drift
An unintended difference between the signed design and the live configuration.
